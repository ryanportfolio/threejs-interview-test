# Decisions

Time spent: [FILL AT SUBMIT] (setup + planning + build; timer started at repo adoption).

- **What I delegated to AI vs. did myself, and why.** Nearly all code generation, plus the planning itself, went to AI: a first-principles pass (decompose / audit assumptions / recombine / test) produced three candidate architectures, and I built the one where correctness under platform rotation is structural rather than computed: the turret is a scene-graph child of the platform, so the controller works in the mount's local frame and "tracking stays correct while the platform rotates" comes from the transform hierarchy, not per-frame bookkeeping. I kept judgment calls for myself: which architecture to build, which reviewer findings to accept or reject, and the interpretation of ambiguous requirements (the 90 deg/s cap is read as a per-axis servo limit in the turret's own frame; platform spin can add apparent world-frame speed, and the one-line alternative, scaling the combined yaw/pitch step to bound total slew, is noted here as the road not taken).

- **Where the AI led me astray and how I caught it.** Two concrete catches, both by verifying against ground truth instead of trusting the reviewer. (1) An AI review panel claimed r185's `worldToLocal` doesn't refresh world matrices and demanded a manual `updateWorldMatrix` call; reading the pinned source (`Object3D.js:679`) refuted it, so the redundant call came out. (2) My own first draft used `THREE.Clock` and `PCFSoftShadowMap`; both are deprecated in r185 and emit console warnings, which would have failed the "no console warnings" bar. A cross-vendor review flagged Clock, the running browser flagged the shadow map, and the fixes (THREE.Timer, PCFShadowMap) were verified against a live console over multiple drone laps. The same live run verified the cap numerically: instrumentation on `window.turretStats` recorded a max joint rate of exactly 90.0 deg/s with sustained saturated frames, so the lag-and-catch-up regime provably engages.

- **What I'd do next with more time.** Extract the controller's `stepJoint`/wrap math into a pure module with a small node assertion suite (the shortest-arc wrap and the 170-degree direction latch are the two spots a reviewer should distrust); tune the drone path so saturation happens in visible bursts on close passes rather than for most of a lap; and give the drone acceleration-limited joints (the current damp-then-cap step is position- and direction-continuous, but angular velocity still has a corner when saturation ends).

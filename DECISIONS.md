# Decisions

Time spent: about 70 min

- **What I delegated vs did myself:** Claude Code (Fable 5) wrote the code. Planning ran through a chain of 4 fresh Claude subagents from /dare (decompose the problem, audit its assumptions against the installed three.js source, recombine, design tests) that produced 3 candidate architectures. The judgment calls I made: **(1)** picked the architecture (turret parented to the rotating platform, so "tracking stays correct while the platform rotates" comes from the scene graph instead of per-frame math), **(2)** triaged ~40 findings from 7 different GPT-5.6 Sol reviews & 5-agent Opus 5 review panel, **(3)** hand-tuned the look through a /lab slider page and pasted the values back, **(4)** and chose the reading of the 90 deg/s cap: it limits the turret's combined yaw+pitch motion relative to its own mount, which satisfies every joint-space reading; the spinning platform can still add world-frame speed, deliberately, because a turret is not responsible for its vehicle's motion.

- **Where AI led me astray, and how I caught it:** **(1)** Opus 5 panel claimed a three.js function (`worldToLocal`) reads stale position data unless refreshed by hand; the installed source (`Object3D.js:679`) shows it refreshes itself. **(2)** The first draft used `THREE.Clock` and `PCFSoftShadowMap`, both deprecated in the pinned three.js r185 and both printing console warnings; Codex caught `Clock`, watching the live console in Chrome caught the shadow map, and both were replaced with supported equivalents (`THREE.Timer`, VSM shadows). **(3)** A Codex review focused on the ground shader caught 2 pieces of GPU code the GLSL spec leaves undefined (`pow()` with a negative base, `atan(0,0)`), the kind that renders fine on one graphics card and glitches on another. **The habit**: take no claim on faith and check the installed source or a live measurement. **The proof**: `window.turretStats` shows the turret pinned at exactly 90.0 deg/s for hundreds of frames, held back by the speed limit. And the head sitting at exactly 0 deg at the moment the drone flew low enough that aiming straight at it would've meant tilting 5.3 deg below horizontal. Both rules got deliberately provoked and both held.

- **Next with more time:** **(1)** Start with my longer multi-session workflow (/long-horizon), which plans, builds, audits, and refines in verified rounds, especially for visuals and perf. **(2)** Pull the tracking math into its own module with unit tests. **(3)** Merge the repeated turret and drone parts into instanced draws (~40 fewer draw calls, same pixels). **(4)** Give the joints an acceleration limit so the turret eases out of full-speed pursuit instead of stopping on a dime when it catches up. **(5)** Automated proof; script plays the scene and fails if the turn rate ever exceeds 90 deg/s or the head dips below horizontal. **(6)** A randomized drone path to show the tracking works for any target, not just this loop.

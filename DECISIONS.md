- **What I delegated vs did myself:** Claude Code (Fable 5) wrote the code. Planning started with /dare, a chain of 4 fresh subagents (decompose the problem, audit assumptions, recombine, design tests) that produced 3 candidate architectures. The judgment calls were mine: <ins>**(1)**</ins> Reviewed the 3 candidates and went with the recommendation: turret parented to the rotating platform, so tracking stays correct while the platform rotates. <ins>**(2)**</ins> Triaged ~40 findings from 7 GPT-5.6 Sol reviews and an Opus workflow. <ins>**(3)**</ins> Hand-tuned the look through a /lab slider page and pasted the values back. <ins>**(4)**</ins> Chose the reading of the 90 deg/s cap. Does it limit the turret's own motors, or the barrel's total sweep through the world including the platform spinning underneath? I went with the motor reading: yaw and pitch combined never exceed 90 deg/s relative to the mount. The platform can still add apparent speed on top, the same way a tank turning doesn't change what its turret's servos can do.

- **Where AI led me astray, and how I caught it:** <ins>**(1)**</ins> The Opus workflow claimed a three.js function reads stale position data unless refreshed by hand; the installed source shows it refreshes itself. <ins>**(2)**</ins> The first draft used `THREE.Clock` and `PCFSoftShadowMap`, both deprecated in the pinned r185 and printing console warnings; Codex caught `Clock`, watching the live console in Chrome caught the shadow map, and both were replaced with supported equivalents. <ins>**(3)**</ins> A Codex review of the ground shader caught 2 pieces of GPU code the GLSL spec leaves undefined; the kind that renders fine on one graphics card and glitches on another. <ins>**(4)**</ins> The first tracking pass capped yaw and pitch separately, so moving both at once could sweep a combined 127 deg/s (113 measured live); a Codex review caught it, and the fix caps the two-axis step as one vector. <ins>**(5)**</ins> The AI's 1st draft of DECISIONS.md misdescribed the build: wrong shadow-map name, and "drone joints" for what are turret joints; the submission review caught both. <ins>**(6)**</ins> The AI overcounted the time spent as "about 70 minutes" - caught myself, prompt timestamps show 1hr exactly.
